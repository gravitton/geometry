## [v1.14.0 (2026-09-20)](https://github.com/gravitton/geometry/compare/v1.13.0...v1.14.0)

### Breaking
- A rotated `Rectangle` answers from its vertices: `Min`, `Max`, `MinMax` and `Bounds` are their axis-aligned extent, `Clamp` snaps to the nearest turned edge, and `DistanceSquaredTo` returns `float64`
- Two rectangles of different angles have no common rectangle: `IntersectionRectangle` returns false and `Union` gives the box around both `Bounds`
- `Vertices` and `Edges` return an `iter.Seq` instead of a slice
- Renames: `Line` is `Segment` with `Seg`, the same-kind `Intersects`/`Intersection` carry the shape name, `Circle.Circumference` is `Perimeter`, the `Polygon.Vertices` field is `Points`, and `Segment.MinMax` and `Polygon.MinMax` are unexported
- A size is signed throughout: `Size.Grow` and `Shrink` no longer clamp at zero, and `Vector.Size` keeps the sign of each component instead of taking it absolute, so it is the inverse of `Size.Vector` like every other conversion between two types; the shapes that store a size take it absolute themselves
- `Segment.MoveTo` places the midpoint, and `Rectangle.Rectangle` spans exactly `Size.Int` pixels
- `Polygon.String` separates vertices with `;`, and `RegularPolygon` omits a zero angle from its `String` and JSON
- `Rectangle.Area` returns `float64`, like every product, so the rectangle is a `Body` beside the curved shapes; `Size.Area` keeps the exact integer area
- `Polygon.Empty` and `RegularPolygon.Empty` are `IsEmpty`, following the `Is` prefix every other zero-argument state check carries
- `FlatTop` and `PointyTop` are `OrientationFlatTop` and `OrientationPointyTop`, prefixed as `OrientationNone` and every `Direction` and `Axis` constant are; `String` and the JSON form stay `FlatTop` and `PointyTop`
- `Segment.Lerp(t)` is `PointAt(t)`, since every other `Lerp` interpolates toward another value of the same type
- `Axis.Cross` is `Perpendicular`, leaving `Cross` to the product on `Vector`
- `Direction.Rotate(steps)` is `Turn(steps)`, leaving `Rotate` to the radian angle it takes on every other type
- `Polygon.Center` is `Centroid`, its textbook name; `Center` stays the stored field on the other shapes
- `Rectangle.Top`, `Bottom`, `Left` and `Right` are removed, and so are `TopEdge`, `RightEdge`, `BottomEdge` and `LeftEdge`: the edge midpoints are `Anchor(Top)` and the other cardinals, the one name for that point on every shape, and `Edges` yields the four edges in the same winding; the corner getters stay, since they are the vertices the edges are built from, and no other shape names an edge or a midpoint

### Added
- `Ellipse` – the continuous shape with two semi-axes and an angle, the target an affine matrix had nowhere to put: `Ell(center, size, angle)` with `Size` holding the semi-axes as on `RegularPolygon`, `SemiMajor`, `SemiMinor`, `Eccentricity` and `Foci`, `Anchor` on the boundary in each of the eight directions, a diagonal one on the diagonal through the center as `Circle.Anchor` places it, `Area`, the Ramanujan `Perimeter` and the closed-form `Bounds` of a turned ellipse, the arithmetic and geometry every shape has, and `Contains`, `DistanceTo` and `DistanceSquaredTo`, which decide the interior on the exact quadratic form and measure to the foot of the perpendicular otherwise, a bisection that ends on a NaN coordinate as it does on any other. It satisfies `Shape`, `Body` and `Transformable`, and deliberately not `Collider`; a zero semi-axis is the degenerate ellipse and answers as the segment it is
- `Circle.Ellipse()` and `Ellipse.Circle()` – the conversion between the two, the ellipse of equal semi-axes one way and the circle around the ellipse, of its major semi-axis, the other, the same shape back where `IsCircle` holds. A circle keeps having no `Transform` of its own: an affine matrix takes it to an ellipse, so `Ellipse().Transform(matrix)` says plainly that the circle is left behind, and every `Transform` in the package returns its own type
- `Ellipse.RegularPolygon(n)`, `Circle.RegularPolygon(n, orientation)`, and `RegularPolygon.Ellipse()` and `Circle()` – the conversion between a curved boundary and the polygon of the wanted resolution, the outline a circle and an ellipse do not have, so its `Vertices` and `Edges` are what draws or walks one. `RegularPolygon` and `Ellipse` hold the same center, semi-axes and angle, so the polygon within the curve is the conversion and both directions round-trip exactly; scale the ellipse by `1/cos(π/n)` first for the polygon about it. Only the circle takes an `Orientation`, since the phase of the first vertex around an ellipse is not representable
- `AssertEllipse` and `AssertAngle`, the angle comparison `AssertRectangle`, `AssertRegularPolygon` and `AssertEllipse` each spelled out, and `Ellipse` with `Ell` in `types/ints` and `types/floats`
- `Rectangle.Transform(matrix)` – applies an affine matrix like every other `Transform`, exact for a turn, a reflection, a move or a scale of the axes, and the nearest rectangle for a shear, which maps it onto a parallelogram; `Polygon().Transform` holds that case
- `Intersects(a, b)` – tests two shapes held as `Collider` by the method naming the kind of the second, the one call a broad collision pass makes over a mixed list
- `Transformable[T, S]` in `shape.go` – the constraint over `Translate`, `MoveTo`, `Rotate`, `Scale` and `Unscale`, each returning the shape's own type, for a generic tween
- `Circle.Rotate(angle)` – the circle itself, since a circle is symmetric about its center, so every shape turns the same way
- `Cast[R]()` on `Point`, `Vector`, `Size`, `Padding`, `Matrix`, `Segment`, `Circle`, `Rectangle`, `Polygon` and `RegularPolygon` – converts to any number type, rounding as `Cast` does, for the types `Int` and `Float` cannot name
- `Rectangle.Angle` – the box of `Size` about `Center`, turned by the angle in the sense of `Vector.Rotate`. `Rect` and the corner constructors leave it zero; `Rotate` turns the rectangle about its center, normalized to `[0, 2π)`; `Lerp` turns along the shorter arc; `Equal`, `IsZero` and `AssertRectangle` compare it modulo a full turn. It marshals as `a` with `omitzero`, so the JSON and `String` of a rectangle that is not rotated are unchanged, and a rotated one prints as `Rect((x,y);WxH;a)`
- `Rectangle.Rotate(angle)` and `IsAligned` – the turn, and the exact test for a rectangle with no turn, which has exact corners and coincides with its `Bounds`
- `Rectangle.Canonical` and `RegularPolygon.Canonical` snap an angle within `Delta` of zero or of a full turn to exactly zero, the residue a chain of turns can leave; `Rotate` and `Lerp` never snap, since a turn that small still moves a far corner of a large shape by more than `Epsilon`
- `Orientation.String`, `MarshalText`, `UnmarshalText`, `ParseOrientation`, `Orientations` and `OrientationNone` with `IsNone`, like `Direction` and `Axis`
- `RegularPolygon.Edges` – the edges of the polygon in vertex order, iterated without building the vertices
- `Segment.Edges` – the one edge of a segment, itself, so a segment is an open outline
- `Shape`, `Outline`, `Collider` and `Body` in `shape.go` – the interfaces every shape satisfies: the point queries `Bounds`, `Contains`, `DistanceTo` and `DistanceSquaredTo`; `Vertices` and `Edges` on the four shapes whose boundary is a chain of edges; the five `Intersects*` tests; and the mass properties `Area`, `Centroid` and `Inertia` at unit density on the five shapes that enclose an area, for a physics body to scale by its density. The outline iterators are free on a concrete shape only, a call through an interface allocates
- `Centroid` on `Rectangle`, `Circle`, `Ellipse` and `RegularPolygon`, returning the `Center` field, beside the existing `Polygon.Centroid`
- `Inertia` on every `Body` – the polar second moment of area about the centroid: closed forms on `Circle`, `Ellipse`, `Rectangle` and `RegularPolygon`, and the per-edge sum moved to the centroid by the parallel axis theorem on `Polygon`, where the moment is the one a simple polygon has: an outline that crosses itself leaves the two sums cancelling by different amounts, and what is left is no longer a moment of area
- `RegularPolygon.Contains`, `DistanceTo` and `DistanceSquaredTo` – the edge walk every closed shape makes, over the edges without building the vertices, so a regular polygon answers exactly what the `Polygon` of its vertices answers
- `RegularPolygon.IntersectsRegularPolygon`, `IntersectsSegment`, `IntersectsRectangle`, `IntersectsCircle` and `IntersectsPolygon`, with `IntersectsRegularPolygon` on `Segment`, `Rectangle`, `Circle` and `Polygon` – every pair of shapes now has `Intersects`, decided as the `Polygon` forms decide it and without building them; `Segment.IntersectionRegularPolygon` and `RegularPolygon.IntersectionSegment` give the boundary crossings the same way
- `RegularPolygon.Anchor(direction)` and `AlignTo(direction, point)` – the point where the ray from the center in that direction leaves the polygon, the midpoint of the top edge of a flat-top polygon and the top vertex of a pointy-top one, and the move that puts it on a point; the direction is a world direction, since the orientation constructors turn the polygon to place its top
- `RegularPolygon.Resize(size)`, `Grow(amount)`, `GrowXY`, `Shrink` and `ShrinkXY` – the semi-axes set, grown and shrunk as on `Ellipse`, clamped at zero and the size taken absolute
- `Size.AtLeastZero()` – each negative extent raised to zero, the clamp `Rectangle`, `Ellipse` and `RegularPolygon` apply after `Grow` and `Shrink`
- `RegularPolygon.IsAligned()` – reports whether the polygon is not turned, its angle exactly zero, the test `Rectangle.IsAligned` and `Ellipse.IsAligned` already make; `String` reads it to decide whether to print the angle

### Changed
- **breaking** `RegularPolygon.Angle` turns the polygon about its center, the meaning it has on `Rectangle`, instead of being a phase added to the polar angle before the semi-axes scaled it. A polygon of equal semi-axes is unchanged, so every `SzU` polygon and every `Triangle`, `Square` and `Hexagon` of a square size keeps its vertices; one with unequal semi-axes and a nonzero angle now turns rather than sliding its vertices along a fixed ellipse, and `Rotate` and `Transform` turn it exactly at any size
- A turned rectangle keeps the names of its corners, edges, anchors and `Inset` paddings from the frame before the turn; `Min`, `Max`, `MinMax` and `Bounds` become the axis-aligned extent of its vertices, and `Clamp` snaps to the nearest turned edge. `Contains`, `DistanceTo` and `IntersectsCircle` walk the edges as `Polygon` does, so a rotated integer rectangle contains exactly what the polygon of its rounded corners contains, and `DistanceSquaredTo` returns `float64` like `Segment.DistanceSquaredTo`, since the nearest point is no longer a lattice point (**breaking**)
- `Rectangle.IntersectsRectangle` is decided on the edges, a corner of one within the other or an edge meeting an edge, with the extent test as a prefilter and an exact overlap of two aligned extents decided at once. Rectangles of the same angle intersect and unite in a rectangle of that angle, found in their shared frame; rectangles of different angles have no rectangle in common, so `IntersectionRectangle` returns false for them, as `Segment.IntersectionSegment` does for parallel segments, and `Union` gives the box around both `Bounds`. `IntersectionRectangle` asks `IntersectsRectangle` first whatever the angles, so for integer `T` the rounded offset in the shared frame never answers where the extent of the rounded corners said no; that frame is also what `IntersectsRectangle` judges two rotated integer rectangles of one angle in, where `IntersectsPolygon` on their rounded corners can differ (**breaking**)
- Every test against a radius or a segment is one squared-distance comparison.
- `Segment.IntersectionCircle` never returns more than two points and counts points that compare `Equal` once; `Circle.IntersectionCircle` places a tangent point halfway between the two boundaries, within half the tolerance of both
- `Segment.MoveTo` places the midpoint on the point, the center every other shape places with `MoveTo` and the pivot its `Scale`, `Resize` and `Rotate` turn about (**breaking**)
- `Rectangle.Rectangle` builds the `image.Rectangle` from `Int`, so it spans exactly `Size.Int` pixels and a sprite keeps its width at sub-pixel positions (**breaking**)
- `Segment.MinMax` and `Polygon.MinMax` are unexported: they are the corners of `Bounds`, so `Bounds().MinMax()` gives the same pair at the same cost; `Rectangle` alone keeps `Min`, `Max` and `MinMax` public (**breaking**)
- `Size.Grow`, `GrowXY`, `Shrink` and `ShrinkXY` no longer clamp at zero, since a size is signed; `Rectangle.Grow` and `Shrink` clamp their own extent as before (**breaking**)
- `Polygon.String` separates the vertices with `;` like every other shape: `Pol((0,0);(2,0);(2,2))`; `RegularPolygon` omits a zero angle from its JSON and `String`, as `Rectangle` does: `RegPol((1,2);2x2;4)` (**breaking**)
- `Polygon.Edges` moved before `Vertices` and `Center`, in the method order every shape follows
- `Vertices` and `Edges` on `Segment`, `Rectangle`, `Polygon` and `RegularPolygon` return an `iter.Seq` instead of a slice, so ranging over an outline allocates nothing; `slices.Collect` gives the slice where one is needed, and the `Polygon()` conversions still build theirs in one allocation. An empty polygon yields nothing where `Edges` returned nil before (**breaking**)
- `Line` is renamed `Segment`, with `Ln` → `Seg`, `IntersectsLine` → `IntersectsSegment`, `IntersectionLine` → `IntersectionSegment`, `DistanceToLine` → `DistanceToSegment`, `DistanceSquaredToLine` → `DistanceSquaredToSegment` and `AssertLine` → `AssertSegment`, in `geom` and in `types/ints` and `types/floats` alike: a value with a `Start` and an `End` is a segment in every geometry library, which is what every doc in the package already called it, and the name leaves `Line` free for the infinite line a ray cast needs. The fields, the JSON keys `s` and `e` and every answer are unchanged; `String` prints `Seg((x,y);(x,y))` (**breaking**)
- The same-kind `Intersects` and `Intersection` carry the shape's name like every mixed pair: `Segment.IntersectsSegment` and `IntersectionSegment`, `Rectangle.IntersectsRectangle` and `IntersectionRectangle`, `Circle.IntersectsCircle` and `IntersectionCircle`, `Polygon.IntersectsPolygon`, `RegularPolygon.IntersectsRegularPolygon`, so the family is one name per pair on every shape (**breaking**)
- `Circle.Circumference` is renamed `Perimeter`, the name every other shape uses (**breaking**)
- `Polygon.Vertices` is renamed `Points`, and `Polygon.Vertices()` iterates it, so `Vertices` and `Edges` are the same API on every shape with an outline. The JSON, a bare array, is unchanged (**breaking**)
- `RegularPolygon.Perimeter` reads the vertex directions from the same expression `Vertices` places them from, so a measure taken without building the vertices cannot drift from them
- `Rectangle`, `Polygon` and `RegularPolygon` share one edge walk, one crossing collector and one edge probe, in `edge.go`, fed by each shape ranging its own `Edges`, so the boundary rule is written once and every edge is prefiltered by its extent; every intersection test rejects a pair by the shapes' extents without placing a rectangle; no answer changes
- Every pair is held by one shape and the other delegates, in the order `Circle`, `Segment`, `Polygon`, `Rectangle`, `RegularPolygon`: `Circle.IntersectsSegment`, `IntersectionSegment`, `IntersectsRectangle`, `IntersectsPolygon` and `IntersectsRegularPolygon` hold the circle pairs, `Segment.IntersectsPolygon` and `IntersectionPolygon` the segment-polygon pair, and the former holders delegate to them; no answer changes
- `Segment.IntersectionRectangle`, `IntersectionPolygon` and their mirrors allocate the result alone, once on the first crossing with room for the two a convex outline can have, and nothing where the segment misses; the shared loop takes the vertices instead of an edge iterator, since an iterator called through a parameter escapes with its loop body
- `Circle.IntersectionSegment` allocates its result once, with room for the two crossings a segment can have, where the second crossing reallocated it before; a segment apart still allocates nothing
- `Segment.chord` decides that a chord is a tangent on the squared chord through `lessOrEqualSquared`, before any square root, where it compared the chord length to `Epsilon[T]()` directly, the one linear comparison against the tolerance the package still had
- `Rectangle.IntersectsRectangle` hands the extents it has already computed to the edge test, and the edge test turns the corners of each rotated rectangle once and probes the edges between them, rather than turning them again for every edge of the other
- `Vector.Less` is judged on the squared length like `LessOrEqual`, with no tolerance as before
- `Rectangle.Anchor`, `Ellipse.Anchor` and `RegularPolygon.Anchor` document that a rounded integer anchor of a turned shape can lie off the boundary, where `Contains` rejects it, as `Circle.Anchor` did
- `RegularPolygon.Anchor` and its test sit before `Vertices`, as on every shape, and the rectangle corner getters read in the winding of `Vertices`
- `Vertices` precedes `Edges` in `Polygon` and `Rectangle`, as it does in `Segment`, `RegularPolygon` and the `Outline` interface, with the tests in the same order
- The package doc lists `Intersects` on two foreign colliders among the panics and `RegularPolygon.Contains` among the closed boundaries, and no doc comment carries a worked numeric example
- Docs and the README name the methods as they are now: `Direction.Turn` in the README snippet, `Vertices` where the winding notes cited a `Points` that `Rectangle` and `RegularPolygon` do not have, and `IntersectsPolygon`, `IntersectionSegment` and `IntersectionRectangle` where the family was still cited bare; the ellipse notes say why the bisection keeps its inside bracket
- Every earlier release with a breaking change opens with a **Breaking** group, as the two latest do, with its entries moved there from `Changed` and `Removed`

### Fixed
- A point with a NaN coordinate is no longer inside every `Rectangle`, `Polygon` and `RegularPolygon`, and a circle centered on one no longer intersects them: the even-odd ray asks for each side of an edge by its own sign, so a NaN crosses nothing
- `Polygon.Area` sums with the origin at the first vertex, as `Centroid` and `Inertia` do, so a polygon far from the origin keeps its area instead of losing it between two large coordinates, and the three agree on the polygon that encloses none
